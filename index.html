What is Climate Change?
Climate scientists have shown that nearly all global warming over the past 200 years is caused by human activities that release greenhouse gases and heat the Earth faster than at any time in the last two thousand years. The planet is now about 1.42°C warmer than in the late 1800s, making this the warmest period in 100,000 years, with 2015 to 2024 being the hottest decade on record. But rising temperatures are only the beginning because the Earth is interconnected, so changes in one area affect many others. As a result, climate change triggers extreme droughts, water scarcity, intense fires, rising sea levels, flooding, melting polar ice, powerful storms, and declining biodiversity.
The Problems We Are Facing
The Earth is facing unprecedented pressure as global temperatures keep rising, leading to extreme heat, unpredictable seasons, melting polar ice, and higher sea levels that threaten coastal cities. Disasters like floods, droughts, and wildfires are becoming more frequent, while pollution harms human health and unpredictable weather puts agriculture and food supplies at risk. Natural ecosystems are losing balance, causing many species to struggle and biodiversity to decline. The climate crisis also strains the economy and society, with damaged infrastructure, rising healthcare costs, and greater impacts on vulnerable communities. Even so, we can still slow the crisis through awareness, lifestyle changes, and collective action to reduce emissions and build a safer, more sustainable future.

Jakarta Flooding (Indonesia)
Jakarta is one of the cities most vulnerable to climate change. Recurrent major floods inundated 300+ locations in 2020, causing fatalities and mass evacuations. Rising rainfall intensity worsens conditions.
Forest Fires in Kalimantan & Sumatra (Indonesia)
Longer, drier seasons have intensified forest fires. In 2019, fires caused haze across Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, damaging ecosystems and affecting millions with respiratory illnesses.
Extreme Heat Waves (Indonesia)
Around 48.6 million people (~17% of Indonesia's population) experienced extreme heat Dec 2024–Feb 2025, linked to fossil fuel-driven global warming.
Floods & Landslides Early 2025 (Indonesia)
Severe flooding and landslides hit many regions in early 2025. Changes in weather patterns from climate extremes increase disaster frequency and intensity.
Arctic Ice Melt (Global)
Arctic and Antarctic ice continues to shrink, reaching the tenth-lowest Arctic extent on record, contributing to global sea level rise and threatening coastal cities worldwide.
Drought in East Africa (Global)
Kenya, Ethiopia, and Somalia face longer, intense dry seasons. Climate change worsens food insecurity and triggers conflicts over scarce resources.
Super Typhoon Haiyan (Philippines)
In 2013, Haiyan, intensified by warmer oceans, killed 6,000+ people, destroyed infrastructure, and left millions homeless.
Record Global Temperatures (Global)
2024 marked the first year global average temperatures reached 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels, surpassing Paris Agreement targets. 2025 projections suggest record heat.

Why the Small Actions We Take Are Important

We often feel that small actions won’t make a difference. However, it is precisely through these simple and small steps that big changes can happen. Reducing single-use plastics, saving energy at home, or choosing eco-friendly transportation may seem trivial, but if done by millions of people, the impact can be significant.
Small actions also have the power to spread. When one person starts taking action, others are inspired and follow suit. From daily habits, a new culture that cares more for the planet emerges. Moreover, small actions demonstrate our commitment: that we are not just waiting for others or the government, but actively taking responsibility for the future.
In other words, every small step is part of a larger movement. The climate crisis is indeed a global problem, but the solution begins with individual choices. If we all act together, small actions will become the foundation for major changes that save the Earth.
